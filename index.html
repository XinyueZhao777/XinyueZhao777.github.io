About

I am a PhD candidate in Finance at the Georgia Institute of Technology. My research studies how financial distress propagates to households and firms, with particular attention to credit access and lender intervention, and how AI agents and machine learning can open new empirical questions in economics and finance.

Before Georgia Tech I earned an MSc in Financial Analysis from London Business School and dual bachelor's degrees in Economics and Arabic from Peking University.

Can LLMs Take the Pulse of the Economy? A Real-Time Evaluation of LLM Nowcasts on Macroeconomic Indicators

Xinyue Zhao, Ruiyi Zhang, Liqin Ye, Rui Cao, Pengtao Xie, Sudheer Chava

We introduce LiveMacroEval, a live, contamination-resistant benchmark for evaluating whether web-enabled LLM agents can nowcast 16 major U.S. macroeconomic indicators before their official release. Using hourly predictions collected over six months, we compare LLM agents with Federal Reserve nowcasts, the Bloomberg professional consensus, and a statistical baseline. LLM agents perform broadly comparably to institutional and professional forecasts.

Working Papers

Retailer Bankruptcy and Its Spillover to Consumer Credit

with Sudheer Chava, Rohan Ganduri, and Yafei Zhang

Using U.S. credit bureau data, we show that retailer bankruptcies lead to widespread retail credit card closures, sharply reducing consumers' credit limits. Credit-constrained households cannot replace this lost credit and face larger, persistent declines in borrowing capacity, resulting in lower credit card borrowing. This reveals a new retail credit card channel through which retailer bankruptcies impose lasting negative externalities on households.

The “Myth” of Covenant Violation Waiver

with Sudheer Chava and Shunlan Fang

Using a fine-tuned RoBERTa model to identify covenant violations and waivers in corporate filings, we show that waivers are granted in 67% of violations, with 22% involving material terms. Relative to comparable violators without waivers, waived firms deleverage but face larger increases in interest expenses, deeper investment cuts, and higher bankruptcy risk. These patterns are consistent with creditor rent extraction rather than waivers serving as pure borrower relief.
